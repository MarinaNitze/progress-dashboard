Though every team member can play a role in maintaining kin connections, without clear ownership over kin-finding, important steps can fall through the cracks.

Formally dedicate someone on your team to be responsible for kin-finding. This creates accountability and helps find placements with kin who can serve as lifelong support for children and youth.

## How to do this

**Figure out where kin-finding fits in your agency**

* Determine the right place for kin-finding responsibility in your agency. A small team may work well when the child's caseworker is also responsible for kin-finding, while another agency may need a focused kin-finding team.
* If you're unsure, run a pilot to try out multiple arrangements and get feedback from employees.

**Map out the process and assign responsibilities**

* Make a process map of the kin-finding process and all its expected steps. Make sure that responsibilities and resources are assigned correctly, and that no steps are skipped in transitions.
* Ask a variety of employees for their feedback. One team may be happy with the current setup, while another team may be frustrated that they regularly find available kin far into a child's case.

**Set up success measures and empower staff**

* Create metrics for success, like the percentage of initial kin placements out of all placements. Roll out new metrics slowly and with plenty of feedback.
* Give team members who are responsible for kin-finding the ability to make changes in other areas of the agency to support their work.

**Make responsibilities official**

* Update position descriptions, performance reviews, and other materials to make responsibilities clear.
* If you have a separate kin-finding team, require that they work with the child, the parents, and the child's worker. Kin-finding can't be done in isolation or without talking to the child and their parents when available.
